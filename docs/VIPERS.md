# Vipers: the trading strategies

Every strategy DRADIS ships, what it trades and how it is gated. To write your own, see [SCRIPTED_VIPERS.md](SCRIPTED_VIPERS.md). How vipers fit into the engine is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Viper Wing (`src/vipers/`)

Twelve specialized Viper strategy classes. Each Viper is an autonomous tactical unit with its own capital budget, position book, and entry/exit logic.

| Viper            | Venue        | Description                                                                                                                                                                               |
|------------------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Momentum**     | Hourly       | Detects high-velocity Binance moves and strikes Polymarket before it reprices                                                                                                             |
| **Maker**        | Window       | Dual-sided passive bids on YES+NO, capturing the spread while managing net exposure                                                                                                       |
| **Arbitrage**    | Window/Daily | Buys both YES+NO when combined asks are < $1.00 (net of fees)                                                                                                                             |
| **Time Decay**   | Hourly       | Posts resting GTC maker bids on both sides during the theta window; a completed pair settles at $1.00. The partner bid rests until the viper's own exits, rotation or close end it (all venues since 1.6.0). The balanced profile enters only when a settled book offers 3¢ or more of theta, sizes $5 a leg and holds a lone leg to settlement rather than stopping it out |
| **Basis**        | Window       | Fades retail skew using Binance funding rates as smart-money confirmation                                                                                                                 |
| **GBoost**       | Hourly       | Engine-trained gradient-boosted model (plan B), fitted as a residual over the market mid and licensed for real money only when it out-forecasts the price on a holdout: scores both sides once a minute from Binance klines and funding, enters as a taker when P(win) clears break-even plus a margin, exits by the configured posture (resting take-profit and taker stop, or held to settlement) |

### Bookline — the sports Viper, and what "trial" means here

**Bookline** is the first Viper that trades something other than crypto. The thesis is narrow on purpose: a vig-free consensus across many bookmakers is a sharper estimate of a game's outcome than a prediction market's thin moneyline book, so a bid resting a few points under that consensus is a bet on the books being right rather than a directional view on the game. It quotes the favorite side only, because de-vigging systematically overstates longshots and a record built on cheap outcomes would measure that artifact instead of the bookmakers' information.

It is maker-first and deliberately timid. It refuses to quote at all unless enough books agree, their dispersion is tight, the consensus is above the favorite floor, and kick-off is still far enough off; it pulls a resting bid the moment the consensus moves against it, since a cancel is free and the asymmetry between an unfilled pull and an adversely-selected fill is the entire reason the strategy can work. The required edge tapers as kick-off approaches and widens when the line is moving. Held to settlement, the exit is fee-free on Polymarket International, so the take-profit and the settle-time decision are priced with each venue's own maker fee — including Polymarket US, where that fee is a rebate and therefore negative.

Two staleness thresholds govern the feed, and the distinction matters: **Max Feed Age To Quote** is how old a consensus may be for Bookline to *place* a bid, while **Max Feed Age To Hold** is how old it may get before an *already-resting* bid is withdrawn. The second is much looser than the first, because committing new capital demands a current line whereas a bid already resting at a price that was current when placed is not made wrong by the feed going quiet. Sharing one threshold capped a quote's life at the entry bar, which is far too short for a passive maker to be crossed. Keep the hold threshold comfortably above the widest gap in the snapshot schedule.

**Status: simulated, and unproven.** Bookline is instantiated and evaluated on every venue like the crypto Vipers, and it runs live on real markets rather than behind instance-wide ghost mode — but it places no venue order. It keeps its own ledger (`bookline_shadow`) recording each quote, pull, fill and settlement so the strategy accumulates a real record against real books before any capital is committed. The venue-matching layer is implemented for all three venues; at the time of writing the Polymarket US path has not yet been exercised against the live gateway, and no settled Bookline record exists on any venue. Treat the numbers it produces as a trial in progress, not as a track record. It is off by default (`BOOKLINE_ENABLED`) and every threshold above is a live `DynamicConfig` knob on the Bookline card in Control Tower.

### Anchor — the sideways-regime Viper, and why it keeps a ledger

**Anchor** exists for the hours the other crypto Vipers sit out: the market drifting a few hundred dollars around its strike with nothing for Momentum or TrendCapture to catch. The research behind it found that pricing an hourly market from an external feed alone is noise near the strike late in the hour, because the venue's settlement index and the oracle feed differ by a basis that moves, and a $15 level error is a quarter of the remaining sigma. So Anchor takes the **level from the market itself**: each tick it inverts the YES mid into the index the market implies, keeps a rolling median of the difference between that and the oracle over the previous one to ten minutes, and uses the oracle only for the **move** since then. Fair value is then a plain gamma read from that anchored level, and Anchor buys the cheap side as a taker when fair clears the ask by the minimum edge net of fee.

It is narrow by design and every gate is a `DynamicConfig` knob on its card. It trades hourly crypto markets only, in the last thirty minutes of the hour but not the last five; it wants an ask between 0.20 and 0.60, the strike within one sigma of the anchored level, realized vol under 35% annualized (above that, the other Vipers have the regime), at least twenty anchor samples spanning five minutes, and a clean order book. One buy per side per hour, a fixed contract count, a ceiling on unsettled notional, and no exit order: the position is held to settlement and the venue sweep books it.

**Status: trial.** Anchor trades through the squadron's dispatcher like every other Viper, so instance-wide ghost mode decides whether its fills are simulated or real. Either way it writes each fill, with the model's reading at that moment, to its own ledger (`anchor_shadow`), and beside it records a **control lane**: the expensive side it refused, so the record shows whether the model is picking sides or just picking cheap contracts. In ghost mode it also books its own simulated settlements, since the venue loops have no settlement path for a simulated position held through an hourly close. The go/no-go is statistical, not anecdotal: roughly 300 hours and 150 entries with a positive average per contract after fees, more hours positive than not, and the control lane negative. Until the ledger says that, a losing hour means nothing and a winning one means nothing. It is off by default (`ENABLE_ANCHOR_TRADING`).

### Helm — the operator in the cockpit, and what "beta" means here

Every other Viper decides for itself. **Helm** is the one that does not: the operator picks the market and the side, and the engine supplies the discipline. It exists because the model-driven Vipers are blind outside their domain. Momentum needs Binance velocity, FairValue needs a strike, GBoost needs its hourly feature set, so a market about a hurricane, an election or a fight has no signal any of them can read. The only signal available there is the operator's own, and Helm's purpose is to make acting on that signal accountable rather than casual.

So the entry is a form, not a button. You state a **thesis**, a **probability**, a **horizon**, and the condition that would prove you **wrong**, and you choose the exit posture: a stop, a take-profit, a time limit, or holding to settlement. The thesis is stored as first written and never changes. The posture is versioned, so you can move a stop as the trade develops and the record still shows what you believed when you committed. That asymmetry is the whole design: a conviction you can quietly rewrite after the outcome is not evidence of anything.

The engine then does four things the operator cannot be relied upon to do. It **validates the posture against the live book**, refusing a stop that is already through the bid or a time limit past the market's close. It requires a posture that **names an exit**, so no position can exist that nothing will close. It **prices the venue fee against the target** and refuses an entry the fee would dominate, which matters more than it sounds: a round trip on Polymarket International costs 11.9% of notional at $0.15 and a posture with no price target has to beat the market by at least that much before it breaks even. And it **asks an LLM to critique the reasoning** before you commit, one shot with a hard timeout, advisory only. The critique is given the thesis, the confidence, the horizon, the falsification condition, the market name and the current price, and deliberately no Raptor state: given the book and the signals it becomes a second model with an opinion on the market, which is not the job. Given only the reasoning, it is a reader who can tell you your falsification condition is unfalsifiable. It cannot refuse a trade and it cannot delay one past its timeout; if the provider is down the intent records `critique unavailable` and proceeds.

A Helm squadron is a **single-member squadron**: one market, one Viper, no Raptors unless its deploy asks for them, its own capital budget and position book. It retires itself once its intents are terminal and it is flat. Whether the critique named the failure that actually happened is recorded against each resolved intent, which over time measures the Advisor rather than flattering it. Calibration, stated confidence against realized outcome, is **recorded from the first intent and displayed from none of them** until thirty have resolved: Helm produces a handful a month, so a hit rate drawn from ten would read as evidence without being any.

**Status: beta. One live trade, on purpose, on Polymarket International.** Helm is tested end to end in ghost mode, including class resolution, the full intent lifecycle, a real entry placed by the strategy, a posture exit, self-retirement and every refusal above. On 2026-10-03 it entered one small real position to prove the live path, and that run taught something ghost mode could not: chain-sync adopts the wallet's whole on-chain balance of the token into the Helm position, so a stray holding the engine had no record of was folded into the intent. Helm then manages shares the conviction never bought, and the intent's cost basis blends them in. If you test with real money, use a token your wallet does not already hold. `HELM_LIVE_ENABLED` ships `false` and is deliberately excluded from the risk profiles, so applying conservative, balanced or aggressive cannot arm it: turning it on is a separate deliberate act, as is `HELM_FEE_VERDICT_ENFORCE`, which ships on because a profile may set how strict a gate is but must never turn one off. **All three venues are wired; only Polymarket International has been exercised.** Helm reads the book and the market through the venue abstraction (`Execution::best_bid`, `best_ask`, `market_facts`), which all three venues implement, so nothing in the Helm API or the strategy names a venue. The deployment runners needed wiring too and now have it: each venue resolves a Helm deployment to the `helm` asset so the squadron carries one viper instead of its market's own class set, aliases the `helm` database shard, and closes the squadron's open intents when its market loop ends. Until that wiring existed, a US deployment was refused as "not a sports market" and a Kalshi one on a crypto ticker scoped itself to `btc`, leaving its intents unwritable and unclosable. Helm has still only ever *executed* on Polymarket International: the US and Kalshi paths compile and are unit-tested but have never run, not even in simulation, so treat them as untested rather than working. Each earns its own ghost acceptance run before `HELM_LIVE_ENABLED` is worth considering there. One behavioral difference to expect: on Polymarket International a Helm squadron retires itself the moment its intents are terminal, because that lifecycle lives in the shared patrol; the other two venues run their own market loops, so a squadron there lives until its market closes or the operator stands it down, and its intents are closed at that point rather than earlier. Two venue differences are worth knowing if you read the records. On Polymarket US the two legs of a market are one book quoted on the long side, so the short leg's prices are the crossed complement (its ask is one minus the long *bid*), and for a game market the legs are the two competitors rather than a yes/no question, which `MarketFacts::leg_labels` carries so the operator is not told they are buying "NO" when they are buying a team. On Kalshi a market publishes both a close and an expected expiration and can close early; the close is used in both the validator and the engine, deliberately the same figure in both, because a validator judging a posture on a number the engine will not act on refuses legitimate entries without catching anything real. One gap worth naming for anyone testing: a resting entry that never fills is unit-tested only, because ghost mode fills a resting bid instantly and the miss cannot be produced locally.

### Build a Viper: your own strategies as scripts

The **Build a Viper** page in Control Tower lets you write a strategy as a short [Rhai](https://rhai.rs) script, save it, and have it run from the next tick, with no rebuild. That makes it the way to add your own Viper on a Marketplace instance. A script defines `entry(ctx)` and `exit(ctx, pos)`, reads the squadron's market, signals and its own parameters, and returns a hold, a buy, a sale or a take-profit. The engine owns every check that protects money, whatever the script returns:

- **Paper by default.** A new viper trades in its own paper ledger and never reaches the venue. Its **Live** switch sends real orders only while the instance is live as well.
- **Limits.** A required exposure cap, counted across every squadron. The daily drawdown limit. Taker limits at most 2¢ past the touch, the venue's minimum size, and one position per market.
- **Exits** that keep running after the viper is switched off.
- **A sandbox** with no file, network or clock access, and a hard bound on each call's work.

Scripts live on the data volume and travel in the instance backup. **Status: new**, covered by unit and end-to-end engine tests. Run your own on paper first. Guide: [SCRIPTED_VIPERS.md](SCRIPTED_VIPERS.md).

**Sharing vipers.** Any script viper exports as a bundle file that another DRADIS can import. The **Community** card on the same page browses the community registry at [dradis.live/vipers](https://dradis.live/vipers). Every version there is signed with a key kept offline, and the engine checks that signature before it shows or imports anything. Whichever way a viper arrives, you review its readme, parameters and full source (with a diff when it replaces one of yours), and it lands **switched off and on paper**. Community vipers are contributed by pull request to [dradis-contrib](https://github.com/mbordash/dradis-contrib) (MIT licensed), checked by CI and reviewed before they are published. They are not investment advice and are not reviewed for profitability.

Building from source and want a compiled Rust Viper instead: [CUSTOM_STRATEGY.md](CUSTOM_STRATEGY.md).
